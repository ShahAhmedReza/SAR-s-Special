# Creative Question: Work, Energy & Power

**[P1-Ch05: Work, Energy & Power | Micro-topic: Work-Energy Theorem | Tier 1 | Level: Knowledge(a)/Comprehension(b)/Application(c)/HigherOrder(d) — CQ]**

---

### Stem
A $2\text{ kg}$ block, initially at rest, is acted upon by a net horizontal force that does $64\text{ J}$ of work on it while it slides across a floor. A technician claims that if instead the same block starts with an initial speed of $3\text{ m/s}$ and the same $64\text{ J}$ of net work is done on it, the final speed will simply be double the case where it started from rest.

---

### Questions & Answers

#### (a) State the Work-Energy Theorem. (1 mark)

The Work-Energy Theorem states that **the net work done on a body equals the change in its kinetic energy, $W_{\text{net}} = \Delta KE = KE_f - KE_i$**.

---

#### (b) Why does only the net work (not work by a single force) determine change in kinetic energy? (2 marks)

Only net work determines the change in kinetic energy because **kinetic energy change results from the net acceleration produced by all forces combined, per Newton's second law, not from any one individual force acting alone**.

If several forces act on a body simultaneously, each does its own work, but the body's actual change in speed depends on the resultant (net) force via $F_{\text{net}} = ma$. Summing the work of each individual force algebraically is equivalent to computing the work of this net force; using only one force's work while other forces are also acting would not correctly predict the true change in kinetic energy.

---

#### (c) Calculate the final speed of the $2\text{ kg}$ block that starts from rest. (3 marks)

The stem directly relates to **Work-Energy Theorem Applied to Find Final Velocity**.

> **Work-Energy Theorem:** $W_{\text{net}} = \frac{1}{2}m v_f^2 - \frac{1}{2}m v_i^2$.

From the stem:
* Mass, $m = 2\text{ kg}$
* Initial velocity, $v_i = 0\text{ m/s}$
* Net work, $W_{\text{net}} = 64\text{ J}$

$$64 = \frac{1}{2}(2)v_f^2 - 0 \implies 64 = v_f^2 \implies v_f = 8\text{ m/s}$$

Therefore, the final speed of the block starting from rest is **$8\text{ m/s}$**, found using **Work-Energy Theorem Applied to Find Final Velocity**.

---

#### (d) Evaluate the technician's claim by finding the final speed for the second case and comparing. (4 marks)

The technician's claim is **incorrect — the final speed in the second case is $\sqrt{73} \approx 8.54\text{ m/s}$, not $16\text{ m/s}$ (which would be double of $8\text{ m/s}$)**.

> **Non-Linear Relationship Between Kinetic Energy and Speed:**  
> Because kinetic energy depends on the square of speed, equal work added to a body does not produce a final speed that is a simple multiple of the no-initial-speed case; the initial kinetic energy already present must be accounted for inside the square-root relationship, not through direct proportion.

From the stem — second case:
* Mass, $m = 2\text{ kg}$
* Initial velocity, $v_i = 3\text{ m/s}$
* Net work, $W_{\text{net}} = 64\text{ J}$

$$W_{\text{net}} = \frac{1}{2}m v_f^2 - \frac{1}{2}m v_i^2$$

$$64 = \frac{1}{2}(2)v_f^2 - \frac{1}{2}(2)(3)^2 = v_f^2 - 9$$

$$v_f^2 = 73 \implies v_f = \sqrt{73} \approx 8.54\text{ m/s}$$

**Comparison:**
* Double of the rest case ($8\text{ m/s}$) would be $16\text{ m/s}$.
* Actual computed value: $\approx 8.54\text{ m/s}$.

Therefore, **the true final speed ($\approx 8.54\text{ m/s}$) is far less than double the rest-case value ($16\text{ m/s}$), because kinetic energy, not speed, adds linearly with work — so the technician's claim is false**.
