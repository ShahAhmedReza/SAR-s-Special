# Chapter 5: Work, Energy and Power
## Micro-Topic 1: Work (কাজ)

---

### 1. Topic Identification & Core Theory (NCTB Standard)

**Micro-Topic Name:** Definition and Mathematical Formulation of Work (কাজের সংজ্ঞা ও গাণিতিক রূপ)

**Deep-Dive Explanation:**

In physics, work is done when a force applied on a body causes displacement in the direction of the force. [পদার্থবিজ্ঞানে, কোনো বস্তুর উপর প্রযুক্ত বল যদি বলের দিকে বস্তুর সরণ ঘটায়, তখন কাজ সম্পন্ন হয়েছে বলা হয়।]
If a force F acts on a body and produces a displacement s, and θ is the angle between the force vector and the displacement vector, work W is defined as the dot product of force and displacement. [যদি একটি বস্তুর উপর বল F ক্রিয়া করে এবং s সরণ ঘটায়, এবং বল ভেক্টর ও সরণ ভেক্টরের মধ্যবর্তী কোণ θ হয়, তবে কাজ W কে বল ও সরণের ডট গুণফল হিসেবে সংজ্ঞায়িত করা হয়।]
Work is a scalar quantity, even though force and displacement are both vectors. [কাজ একটি স্কেলার রাশি, যদিও বল ও সরণ উভয়ই ভেক্টর রাশি।]
This is because the dot product of two vectors always yields a scalar. [কারণ দুটি ভেক্টরের ডট গুণফল সর্বদা একটি স্কেলার রাশি প্রদান করে।]
If no displacement occurs, or if the force is perpendicular to displacement (θ = 90°), the work done is zero. [যদি কোনো সরণ না ঘটে, অথবা বল সরণের সাথে লম্ব হয় (θ = 90°), তাহলে কৃতকাজ শূন্য হয়।]
For example, a person holding a heavy bag while standing still does zero work in the physics sense, despite muscular effort. [উদাহরণস্বরূপ, একজন ব্যক্তি স্থির দাঁড়িয়ে ভারী ব্যাগ ধরে রাখলে পদার্থবিজ্ঞানের সংজ্ঞা অনুযায়ী তার কাজ শূন্য, যদিও পেশির শ্রম ব্যয় হচ্ছে।]

**Multi-Book Synthesis:**

- **Tapan Sir (Hasan Book House):** Defines work strictly through the dot-product formalism, W = F⃗·s⃗ = Fs cosθ, and emphasizes that "work" in physics has a restrictive technical meaning distinct from everyday usage — physical effort without displacement is not physics-work. He stresses the scalar nature of W as the foundational conceptual anchor before any numerical work is attempted.
- **Ishaq & Amir Hossain Khan Sir (Ideal Books):** Build the formula from first principles using a force-displacement diagram, deriving W = Fs cosθ by resolving F into components along and perpendicular to displacement (F cosθ does work; F sinθ does none). This book is the primary source for the structured numerical problem-solving approach used in Section 3 below.
- **Akkharpatra Physics:** Adds application-based variety — inclined plane problems, pulling/pushing at an angle, and real-life scenario discussions (e.g., work done climbing stairs vs. carrying a load horizontally), useful for building intuition for CQ (Creative Question) conceptual parts.

### 2. Mathematical Framework & Formulas

**Primary Formula:**

$$W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{s} = Fs\cos\theta$$

| Symbol | Meaning | SI Unit | Physical Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| W | Work done | Joule (J) [জুল] | Energy transferred by the force [বল দ্বারা স্থানান্তরিত শক্তি] |
| F | Applied force | Newton (N) [নিউটন] | Magnitude of the force vector [বল ভেক্টরের মান] |
| s | Displacement | Metre (m) [মিটার] | Distance moved in a straight line, in the direction of net motion [সরল রেখায় প্রকৃত অবস্থান পরিবর্তন] |
| θ | Angle between F and s | Degree/Radian [ডিগ্রি/রেডিয়ান] | Determines the effective component of force doing work [বলের কার্যকর উপাংশ নির্ধারণ করে] |

**Special Cases:**
- θ = 0°: W = Fs (maximum positive work) [সর্বোচ্চ ধনাত্মক কাজ]
- θ = 90°: W = 0 (no work) [কোনো কাজ নেই]
- θ = 180°: W = −Fs (maximum negative work, e.g., friction opposing motion) [সর্বোচ্চ ঋণাত্মক কাজ]

**Dimensional Formula:** [ML²T⁻²] — identical to energy, confirming work-energy equivalence.

**Common Traps (English Version students):**
1. **Sign error:** Students forget that θ > 90° gives negative work — commonly missed with friction/retarding forces problems.
2. **Unit confusion:** Mixing Joule (J = N·m = kg·m²·s⁻²) with Newton or Watt in board answers — always show unit derivation once per question.
3. **cos vs sin mix-up:** Using sinθ instead of cosθ when resolving force along displacement — remember, it is always the *cosine* of the angle *between* F and s, never the angle from the vertical unless explicitly stated.
4. **Confusing "work done by" vs "work done against":** Work done against gravity/friction is reported as positive magnitude of the opposing work, but in the vector formalism it is negative work done *by* that force — board answers must state which convention is being used.

**Type A: Direct Substitution (θ given, F & s given)**

- **Question (Board-pattern, typical of 2017–2022 papers):** A force of 50 N acts on a box at an angle of 60° to the horizontal and moves it 10 m along the horizontal floor. Find the work done.
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. Given: F = 50 N, s = 10 m, θ = 60° [প্রদত্ত মান বসানো]
  2. Formula: W = Fs cosθ [সূত্র প্রয়োগ]
  3. W = 50 × 10 × cos60° = 500 × 0.5 = **250 J**
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:**
  - Step 1 identifies which quantities are directly usable in the formula without conversion. [ধাপ ১ এ কোন রাশিগুলো রূপান্তর ছাড়াই সরাসরি ব্যবহারযোগ্য তা চিহ্নিত করা হয়।]
  - Step 3 uses cos60° = 0.5 exactly, a standard trig value expected to be memorized for board speed. [ধাপ ৩ এ cos60° = 0.5 এই সুনির্দিষ্ট মান ব্যবহার করা হয়, যা বোর্ড পরীক্ষায় দ্রুত সমাধানের জন্য মুখস্থ থাকা প্রত্যাশিত।]

**Type B: Ratio-Based**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** Two forces F₁ and F₂ act on two identical bodies causing equal displacement s. If F₁:F₂ = 3:2 and both act along the direction of motion, find the ratio of work done.
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. Since θ = 0° for both, W = Fs. [উভয় ক্ষেত্রে θ = 0°, তাই W = Fs]
  2. W₁/W₂ = F₁s/F₂s = F₁/F₂ = 3/2 [s বাতিল হয়ে যায়]
  3. **Ratio = 3:2**
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** Because displacement s is common to both cases, it cancels algebraically, reducing the ratio problem to a pure force ratio — a frequently tested board shortcut. [যেহেতু সরণ s উভয় ক্ষেত্রে সমান, বীজগণিতগতভাবে তা বাতিল হয়ে যায়, ফলে সমস্যাটি একটি নিখাদ বল অনুপাতে পরিণত হয় — যা বোর্ডে প্রায়ই আসে।]

**Type C: Negative Work / Opposing Force (Friction)**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** A block is pulled 5 m against a frictional force of 8 N acting opposite to motion. Calculate the work done by friction.
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. θ between friction and displacement = 180°. [ঘর্ষণ বল ও সরণের মধ্যে কোণ 180°]
  2. W = Fs cos180° = 8 × 5 × (−1) = **−40 J**
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** The negative sign is not optional — board markers deduct marks for reporting only the magnitude 40 J without the negative sign when the question asks for "work done by friction" specifically (as opposed to "work done against friction"). [ঋণাত্মক চিহ্নটি ঐচ্ছিক নয় — শুধুমাত্র মান 40 J লিখলে নম্বর কর্তন হয়, যখন প্রশ্নে সুনির্দিষ্টভাবে "ঘর্ষণ দ্বারা কৃতকাজ" জিজ্ঞাসা করা হয়।]

### 4. Micro-Topic Formative Exercise

**Q1 (Conceptual CQ style):** Explain, with a suitable example, why a person carrying a load horizontally at constant velocity does zero work according to physics, even though they feel fatigued. Use the formula W = Fs cosθ in your explanation.

**Answer: The upward supporting force is perpendicular to horizontal displacement, so θ = 90° and W = 0.**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

The force exerted by the person to support the load acts vertically upward (against gravity), while the displacement of the load is horizontal. The angle between these two vectors is θ = 90°. Since cos90° = 0, W = Fs cos90° = 0, regardless of how large F or s is. The fatigue felt is due to continuous muscular (biological) energy expenditure at the cellular level, not mechanical work in the physics sense.

</details>

**Q2 (Mathematical):** A crate is pushed 12 m across a floor by a force of 75 N applied at 40° above the horizontal. Find the work done by the applied force. (Use cos40° ≈ 0.766)

**Answer: W ≈ 689.4 J**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

W = Fs cosθ = 75 × 12 × cos40° = 900 × 0.766 = 689.4 J

</details>

**Q3 (Mathematical, board-difficulty 2024–2026 style):** A force F = (4î + 3ĵ) N acts on a particle, producing a displacement s = (2î − 5ĵ) m. Determine the work done by the force.

**Answer: W = −7 J**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

Using the dot product component-wise:
W = F⃗·s⃗ = (4)(2) + (3)(−5) = 8 − 15 = −7 J

The negative result indicates the force has a net decelerating (opposing) effect along the actual path of displacement despite acting at an angle.

</details>
