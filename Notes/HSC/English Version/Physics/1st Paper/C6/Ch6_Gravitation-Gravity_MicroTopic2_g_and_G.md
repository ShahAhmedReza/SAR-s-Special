# Chapter 6: Gravitation and Gravity
## Micro-Topic 2: Acceleration Due to Gravity (g) and Its Relation to G (অভিকর্ষজ ত্বরণ ও G-এর সাথে সম্পর্ক)

---

### 1. Topic Identification & Core Theory (NCTB Standard)

**Micro-Topic Name:** Deriving g = GM/R² — Connecting the Universal Constant to Earth's Local Gravity (g = GM/R² প্রতিপাদন — সর্বজনীন ধ্রুবক থেকে পৃথিবীর স্থানীয় অভিকর্ষে সংযোগ)

**Deep-Dive Explanation:**

Acceleration due to gravity (g) is the acceleration experienced by any object due to the Earth's gravitational pull, and unlike the universal constant G, g is not fixed everywhere — it depends on the mass and radius of the attracting body. [অভিকর্ষজ ত্বরণ (g) হলো পৃথিবীর মহাকর্ষীয় আকর্ষণের কারণে যেকোনো বস্তুর অভিজ্ঞ ত্বরণ, এবং সর্বজনীন ধ্রুবক G-এর বিপরীতে, g সর্বত্র স্থির নয় — এটি আকর্ষণকারী বস্তুর ভর ও ব্যাসার্ধের উপর নির্ভর করে।]
By Newton's second law, the gravitational force on an object of mass m at the Earth's surface is F = mg; by Newton's law of gravitation, this same force is also F = GMm/R², where M is Earth's mass and R is Earth's radius. [নিউটনের দ্বিতীয় সূত্র অনুসারে, পৃথিবীর পৃষ্ঠে ভর m-এর একটি বস্তুর উপর মহাকর্ষীয় বল F = mg; নিউটনের মহাকর্ষ সূত্র অনুসারে, এই একই বল F = GMm/R²-ও বটে, যেখানে M পৃথিবীর ভর এবং R পৃথিবীর ব্যাসার্ধ।]
Equating these two expressions for the same force and canceling the test mass m from both sides yields g = GM/R², showing that g depends only on the properties of the attracting body (Earth), not on the mass of the object experiencing the acceleration. [একই বলের জন্য এই দুটি রাশিকে সমান করে এবং উভয় পাশ থেকে পরীক্ষণ ভর m বাতিল করলে পাওয়া যায় g = GM/R², যা দেখায় যে g কেবল আকর্ষণকারী বস্তুর (পৃথিবীর) বৈশিষ্ট্যের উপর নির্ভর করে, ত্বরণ অনুভবকারী বস্তুর ভরের উপর নয়।]
This mass-independence is precisely why all objects, regardless of their own mass, fall with the same acceleration in a vacuum (ignoring air resistance) — a conceptually crucial result first demonstrated experimentally by Galileo, and later explained theoretically by Newton's law of gravitation. [এই ভর-নিরপেক্ষতাই ঠিক কারণ কেন সকল বস্তু, তাদের নিজস্ব ভর নির্বিশেষে, শূন্যস্থানে একই ত্বরণে পতিত হয় (বায়ু প্রতিরোধ উপেক্ষা করে) — একটি ধারণাগতভাবে গুরুত্বপূর্ণ ফলাফল যা প্রথম পরীক্ষামূলকভাবে গ্যালিলিও প্রদর্শন করেছিলেন, এবং পরে নিউটনের মহাকর্ষ সূত্র দ্বারা তাত্ত্বিকভাবে ব্যাখ্যা করা হয়।]
Since g = GM/R², any measured local value of g, combined with known values of G and R, allows the mass of the Earth (or any other astronomical body) to be calculated — this was historically one of the most significant applications of Cavendish's measurement of G, sometimes described as "weighing the Earth." [যেহেতু g = GM/R², G ও R-এর জানা মান সহ যেকোনো পরিমাপকৃত স্থানীয় g-এর মান দিয়ে পৃথিবী (বা অন্য যেকোনো জ্যোতির্বৈজ্ঞানিক বস্তু)-র ভর গণনা করা যায় — এটি ঐতিহাসিকভাবে ক্যাভেন্ডিশের G পরিমাপের অন্যতম গুরুত্বপূর্ণ প্রয়োগ ছিল, যাকে কখনো কখনো "পৃথিবীর ওজন মাপা" বলে বর্ণনা করা হয়।]

**Multi-Book Synthesis:**

- **Tapan Sir (Hasan Book House):** Emphasizes the conceptual chain G (universal, fixed) → g (local, variable, depends on M and R of the specific body) → weight (W = mg, depends additionally on the specific object's mass) as the clearest way to prevent the G-vs-g confusion flagged in Micro-Topic 1's Common Traps, and frequently appears as a CQ theme distinguishing mass (invariant) from weight (location-dependent, since g varies).
- **Ishaq & Amir Hossain Khan Sir (Ideal Books):** Provide the full step-by-step equate-and-cancel derivation (F=mg and F=GMm/R² → g=GM/R²) as the standard reproducible board proof, and this book is the primary source for numerical problems calculating Earth's mass from given g, G, R values, or comparing g on different planets given their M and R.
- **Akkharpatra Physics:** Offers application variety comparing g values across celestial bodies (Moon, Mars, Jupiter) using their respective M and R values, and problems relating weight differences of the same object on different planets/moons to the g = GM/R² formula, a favored comparative CQ style in board sets.

### 2. Mathematical Framework & Formulas

**Primary Derivation and Formula:**

$$mg = \frac{GMm}{R^2} \implies g = \frac{GM}{R^2}$$

**Application — Finding Mass of a Planet from g:**

$$M = \frac{gR^2}{G}$$

**Weight of an Object (distinguishing from mass):**

$$W = mg$$

| Symbol | Meaning | SI Unit | Physical Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| g | Acceleration due to gravity (local) | m/s² [মিটার/সেকেন্ড²] | Local gravitational acceleration, varies by body/location [স্থানীয় মহাকর্ষীয় ত্বরণ, বস্তু/স্থান অনুযায়ী পরিবর্তনশীল] |
| G | Universal gravitational constant | N·m²/kg² [নিউটন·মি²/কেজি²] | Fixed everywhere in the universe [মহাবিশ্বে সর্বত্র স্থির] |
| M | Mass of the attracting body (e.g., Earth) | Kilogram (kg) [কিলোগ্রাম] | Source mass creating the gravitational field [মহাকর্ষীয় ক্ষেত্র সৃষ্টিকারী উৎস ভর] |
| R | Radius of the attracting body | Metre (m) [মিটার] | Distance from center to surface [কেন্দ্র থেকে পৃষ্ঠ পর্যন্ত দূরত্ব] |
| m | Mass of the object experiencing gravity | Kilogram (kg) [কিলোগ্রাম] | Test/object mass — cancels out of the g formula [পরীক্ষণ/বস্তু ভর — g সূত্র থেকে বাতিল হয়ে যায়] |
| W | Weight of the object | Newton (N) [নিউটন] | Force experienced due to gravity — location-dependent [মহাকর্ষের কারণে অনুভূত বল — স্থান-নির্ভর] |

**Common Traps (English Version students):**
1. **Confusing mass and weight:** Mass (kg) is an invariant, intrinsic property of an object and does not change with location; weight (N) is a force that depends on local g and therefore changes from planet to planet, or even slightly across Earth's surface — this conceptual pair is one of the most frequently tested distinctions in this chapter.
2. **Forgetting that g is independent of the falling object's own mass:** Students sometimes think a heavier object should have a "larger g" — but g = GM/R² depends only on the attracting body's M and R, never on the mass of the object experiencing the acceleration.
3. **Using Earth's g (9.8 m/s²) by default for other planets:** When a problem specifies a different planet or moon (e.g., Moon's g ≈ 1.62 m/s²), forgetting to use the planet-specific g value, or forgetting to first calculate it from the given M and R if not directly stated.
4. **Unit and order-of-magnitude errors:** M and R for Earth are enormous numbers (M ≈ 5.97×10²⁴ kg, R ≈ 6.37×10⁶ m) — students often make exponent-arithmetic mistakes when substituting into g = GM/R² or M = gR²/G.

### 3. Chronological Type-Wise Board Questions (2016 – 2026)

⚠️ Board-pattern representative problems, not guaranteed verbatim board transcriptions.

**Type A: Direct Substitution (Verify g for Earth)**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** Given Earth's mass M = 5.97×10²⁴ kg and radius R = 6.37×10⁶ m, calculate the acceleration due to gravity at Earth's surface. (G = 6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²)
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. Formula: g = GM/R² [সূত্র প্রয়োগ]
  2. g = (6.674×10⁻¹¹ × 5.97×10²⁴)/(6.37×10⁶)²
  3. g = (3.984×10¹⁴)/(4.058×10¹³) ≈ **9.82 m/s²**
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** Step 3 confirms the derivation matches the commonly known value g ≈ 9.8 m/s², validating the formula and providing a good "sanity check" habit for board numerical answers. [ধাপ ৩ প্রতিপাদনটি সুপরিচিত মান g ≈ 9.8 m/s²-এর সাথে মিলে যাচ্ছে তা নিশ্চিত করে।]

**Type B: Reverse Application (Find Mass from g)**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** The Moon has a radius of 1.74×10⁶ m and a surface gravitational acceleration of 1.62 m/s². Find the mass of the Moon. (G = 6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²)
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. Formula: M = gR²/G [সূত্র পুনর্বিন্যাস]
  2. M = (1.62 × (1.74×10⁶)²)/(6.674×10⁻¹¹) = (1.62 × 3.0276×10¹²)/(6.674×10⁻¹¹)
  3. M = (4.905×10¹²)/(6.674×10⁻¹¹) ≈ **7.35×10²² kg**
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** Step 1 requires algebraic rearrangement of g=GM/R² to isolate M — a frequently tested reverse-application variant, directly relevant to the "weighing a planet" application discussed in Section 1. [ধাপ ১ M বিচ্ছিন্ন করতে বীজগাণিতিক পুনর্বিন্যাস প্রয়োজন।]

**Type C: Comparative (Weight on Different Planets)**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** An astronaut weighs 700 N on Earth (g_Earth = 9.8 m/s²). If Mars has a surface gravity of 3.71 m/s², find the astronaut's weight on Mars, and their mass (which remains constant).
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. Mass (invariant): m = W_Earth/g_Earth = 700/9.8 ≈ 71.43 kg [ভর নির্ণয় — অপরিবর্তনীয়]
  2. Weight on Mars: W_Mars = m × g_Mars = 71.43 × 3.71 ≈ **265 N**
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** Step 1 establishes the mass as the invariant link between the two planetary scenarios — this two-step structure (find mass first, then apply to new location) is the standard approach for all comparative weight problems, directly testing Common Trap #1. [ধাপ ১ ভরকে দুটি গ্রহীয় পরিস্থিতির মধ্যে অপরিবর্তনীয় সংযোগ হিসেবে প্রতিষ্ঠিত করে।]

### 4. Micro-Topic Formative Exercise

**Q1 (Conceptual CQ style):** A student states, "An object has more gravity when it's more massive." Critically evaluate this statement using the distinction between g and G, and clarify what physical quantity actually changes when an object's mass increases.

**Answer: The statement conflates two different ideas — a more massive object does exert a stronger gravitational force (and would produce a larger g if it were the *attracting* body), but a more massive object does not itself "have more gravity" in the sense of experiencing a different g when it is the object *being attracted*; g depends on the attracting body's M and R, not on the mass of the object placed within that gravitational field.**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

The word "gravity" is ambiguous in casual usage and must be separated into two precise physical roles. If an object is the *source* of a gravitational field (e.g., a planet), then yes — increasing its mass M does increase the gravitational acceleration g = GM/R² it produces at its surface (assuming R stays fixed), and it does exert a proportionally stronger attractive force on any nearby object (F = GMm/R²). However, if the same object is instead the one being *acted upon* by gravity (e.g., a person standing on that planet), then that object's own mass has zero effect on the acceleration g it experiences — g is entirely determined by the properties of the attracting body. A heavier person and a lighter person standing on the same planet experience exactly the same g, even though the heavier person experiences a proportionally larger *force* (weight = mg), because their masses differ. This is precisely why all objects fall with the same acceleration in the absence of air resistance, regardless of their own mass.

</details>

**Q2 (Mathematical):** Jupiter has a mass of 1.898×10²⁷ kg and a radius of 6.99×10⁷ m. Find the acceleration due to gravity at Jupiter's surface. (G = 6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²)

**Answer: g ≈ 25.9 m/s²**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

g = GM/R² = (6.674×10⁻¹¹ × 1.898×10²⁷)/(6.99×10⁷)²
= (1.2668×10¹⁷)/(4.886×10¹⁵)
≈ 25.93 m/s²

</details>

**Q3 (Mathematical, board-difficulty 2024–2026 style):** A planet has the same mass as Earth but twice Earth's radius. Find the acceleration due to gravity at this planet's surface as a multiple of Earth's g, and calculate the weight of a 60 kg astronaut on this planet. (g_Earth = 9.8 m/s²)

**Answer: g_planet = g_Earth/4 = 2.45 m/s²; Weight = 147 N**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

g = GM/R²; since M is unchanged but R is doubled (R_planet = 2R_Earth):

g_planet = GM/(2R_Earth)² = GM/(4R_Earth²) = (1/4)(GM/R_Earth²) = (1/4)g_Earth

g_planet = 9.8/4 = 2.45 m/s²

Weight = m × g_planet = 60 × 2.45 = 147 N

(Note: this problem tests the inverse-square dependence of g on R — doubling R quarters g, mirroring the inverse-square logic from Micro-Topic 1's gravitational force ratio problems.)

</details>
