# Chapter 6: Gravitation and Gravity
## Micro-Topic 1: Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation (নিউটনের সর্বজনীন মহাকর্ষ সূত্র)

---

### 1. Topic Identification & Core Theory (NCTB Standard)

**Micro-Topic Name:** Newton's Law of Gravitation — Statement, Formula, and Vector Nature (নিউটনের মহাকর্ষ সূত্র — বিবৃতি, সূত্র ও ভেক্টর প্রকৃতি)

**Deep-Dive Explanation:**

Newton's law of universal gravitation states that every particle of matter in the universe attracts every other particle with a force directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centers. [নিউটনের সর্বজনীন মহাকর্ষ সূত্র অনুসারে, মহাবিশ্বের প্রতিটি বস্তুকণা প্রতিটি অন্য বস্তুকণাকে এমন একটি বলের সাহায্যে আকর্ষণ করে যা তাদের ভরের গুণফলের সমানুপাতিক এবং তাদের কেন্দ্রের মধ্যবর্তী দূরত্বের বর্গের ব্যস্তানুপাতিক।]
This is expressed mathematically as F = Gm₁m₂/r², where G is the universal gravitational constant, a fundamental constant of nature with the same value everywhere in the universe. [এটি গাণিতিকভাবে প্রকাশ করা হয় F = Gm₁m₂/r² হিসেবে, যেখানে G হলো সর্বজনীন মহাকর্ষীয় ধ্রুবক, প্রকৃতির একটি মৌলিক ধ্রুবক যার মান মহাবিশ্বের সর্বত্র একই।]
The gravitational force is always attractive, never repulsive, distinguishing it from electric and magnetic forces which can be either attractive or repulsive depending on charge/pole sign. [মহাকর্ষীয় বল সর্বদা আকর্ষণধর্মী, কখনো বিকর্ষণধর্মী নয়, যা একে তড়িৎ ও চৌম্বক বল থেকে আলাদা করে, যা চার্জ/মেরুর চিহ্নের উপর নির্ভর করে আকর্ষণ বা বিকর্ষণ উভয়ই হতে পারে।]
By Newton's third law, the two bodies exert equal and opposite gravitational forces on each other — the force body 1 exerts on body 2 is equal in magnitude but opposite in direction to the force body 2 exerts on body 1, even if their masses are vastly different. [নিউটনের তৃতীয় সূত্র অনুসারে, দুটি বস্তু পরস্পরের উপর সমান ও বিপরীতমুখী মহাকর্ষীয় বল প্রয়োগ করে — বস্তু ১ যে বল বস্তু ২-এর উপর প্রয়োগ করে তা মানে বস্তু ২ যে বল বস্তু ১-এর উপর প্রয়োগ করে তার সমান কিন্তু দিকে বিপরীত, যদিও তাদের ভর ব্যাপকভাবে ভিন্ন হতে পারে।]
The gravitational constant G was first experimentally measured by Henry Cavendish using a torsion balance, and its extremely small numerical value (6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²) explains why gravitational attraction between everyday objects is imperceptibly weak compared to gravitational attraction involving astronomical masses. [মহাকর্ষীয় ধ্রুবক G প্রথম পরীক্ষামূলকভাবে পরিমাপ করেছিলেন হেনরি ক্যাভেন্ডিশ একটি টর্শন ব্যালেন্স ব্যবহার করে, এবং এর অত্যন্ত ক্ষুদ্র সাংখ্যিক মান (6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²) ব্যাখ্যা করে কেন দৈনন্দিন বস্তুর মধ্যে মহাকর্ষীয় আকর্ষণ জ্যোতির্বৈজ্ঞানিক ভরের তুলনায় অনুভবের অতীত দুর্বল।]

**Multi-Book Synthesis:**

- **Tapan Sir (Hasan Book House):** Emphasizes the universality of the law — it applies identically to an apple falling from a tree and to planets orbiting the sun — as the conceptual breakthrough that unified terrestrial and celestial mechanics, a frequently tested CQ theme about the historical/conceptual significance of Newton's law.
- **Ishaq & Amir Hossain Khan Sir (Ideal Books):** Provide the formal vector form of the law, F⃗ = −Gm₁m₂/r² r̂ (with the negative sign indicating attraction, directed along the line joining the two masses), and this is the primary source for numerical problems involving force direction/superposition when more than two masses are involved.
- **Akkharpatra Physics:** Offers application variety — problems comparing gravitational force magnitudes at different distances (inverse-square scaling), multi-body systems where net gravitational force must be found via vector addition, and conceptual comparisons between gravitational and electrostatic force laws (both inverse-square, but one always attractive).

**A Note on This Chapter's Naming:** In some editions, "Gravitation" (মহাকর্ষ) refers to the general law of attraction between any two masses (this micro-topic), while "Gravity" (অভিকর্ষ) specifically refers to the special case of Earth's gravitational pull on objects near or on its surface (covered starting Micro-Topic 2). This distinction itself is a common conceptual exam point.

### 2. Mathematical Framework & Formulas

**Primary Formula (scalar magnitude):**

$$F = \frac{Gm_1m_2}{r^2}$$

**Vector Form:**

$$\vec{F} = -\frac{Gm_1m_2}{r^2}\hat{r}$$

| Symbol | Meaning | SI Unit | Physical Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| F | Gravitational force between the two masses | Newton (N) [নিউটন] | Magnitude of mutual attraction [পারস্পরিক আকর্ষণের মান] |
| G | Universal gravitational constant | N·m²/kg² [নিউটন·মি²/কেজি²] | Fixed constant of nature ≈ 6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² [প্রকৃতির স্থির ধ্রুবক] |
| m₁, m₂ | Masses of the two interacting bodies | Kilogram (kg) [কিলোগ্রাম] | Source of gravitational attraction [মহাকর্ষীয় আকর্ষণের উৎস] |
| r | Distance between the centers of the two masses | Metre (m) [মিটার] | Separation determining inverse-square scaling [ব্যস্তবর্গীয় স্কেলিং নির্ধারণকারী দূরত্ব] |

**Common Traps (English Version students):**
1. **Confusing G (gravitational constant) with g (acceleration due to gravity):** These are entirely different quantities with different units and different physical meanings — G is universal and constant everywhere, while g varies by location and depends on the local mass and radius (covered in Micro-Topic 2). This is the single most common notational error in this chapter.
2. **Forgetting the inverse-square relationship in ratio problems:** Doubling the distance r reduces the force to 1/4, not 1/2 — students frequently apply a linear (not squared) inverse relationship.
3. **Ignoring the vector/direction requirement in multi-body problems:** When more than two masses are present, forces must be added as vectors (accounting for direction), not simply summed as scalars.
4. **Using non-SI units without conversion:** Distances given in km, or masses in grams, must be converted to metres and kilograms respectively before substitution into F = Gm₁m₂/r² — a frequent source of order-of-magnitude errors.

### 3. Chronological Type-Wise Board Questions (2016 – 2026)

⚠️ Board-pattern representative problems, not guaranteed verbatim board transcriptions — upload actual board PDFs for exact-wording re-solving.

**Type A: Direct Substitution**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** Find the gravitational force between two masses of 50 kg and 80 kg placed 2 m apart. (G = 6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²)
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. Formula: F = Gm₁m₂/r² [সূত্র প্রয়োগ]
  2. F = (6.674×10⁻¹¹ × 50 × 80)/(2²) = (6.674×10⁻¹¹ × 4000)/4
  3. F = (2.6696×10⁻⁷)/4 ≈ **6.674×10⁻⁸ N**
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** Step 3 demonstrates the extremely small magnitude of gravitational force between everyday-scale masses, reinforcing the conceptual point (Section 1) about G's tiny value. [ধাপ ৩ দৈনন্দিন-স্কেল ভরের মধ্যে মহাকর্ষীয় বলের অত্যন্ত ক্ষুদ্র মান প্রদর্শন করে।]

**Type B: Ratio-Based (Inverse-Square Scaling)**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** If the distance between two masses is tripled, find the new gravitational force as a fraction of the original force.
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. F ∝ 1/r² [ব্যস্তবর্গীয় সম্পর্ক]
  2. F_new/F_old = (r_old/r_new)² = (1/3)² = **1/9**
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** Step 2 directly tests the inverse-square relationship — tripling distance means the force becomes 1/9th, not 1/3rd, addressing Common Trap #2 directly. [ধাপ ২ ব্যস্তবর্গীয় সম্পর্ক সরাসরি পরীক্ষা করে।]

**Type C: Multi-Body (Vector Addition)**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** Two masses of 10 kg each are placed on either side of a 5 kg mass, at distances of 2 m and 3 m respectively, all in a straight line. Find the net gravitational force on the 5 kg mass. (G = 6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²)
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. Force from first 10 kg mass (at 2 m): F₁ = G(10)(5)/(2²) = G×50/4 = 12.5G [প্রথম বল নির্ণয়]
  2. Force from second 10 kg mass (at 3 m): F₂ = G(10)(5)/(3²) = G×50/9 ≈ 5.56G [দ্বিতীয় বল নির্ণয়]
  3. Since both masses are on opposite sides and pull the 5 kg mass in opposite directions, net force = F₁ − F₂ = G(12.5 − 5.56) = 6.94G ≈ 6.94 × 6.674×10⁻¹¹ ≈ **4.63×10⁻¹⁰ N**, directed toward the closer (2 m) mass.
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** Step 3 requires recognizing that the two forces act in opposite directions (since the masses are on opposite sides) and must be subtracted, not added — directly testing the vector-addition requirement from Common Trap #3. [ধাপ ৩ বিপরীতমুখী বল বিয়োগ করার প্রয়োজনীয়তা পরীক্ষা করে।]

### 4. Micro-Topic Formative Exercise

**Q1 (Conceptual CQ style):** A student argues that since the Earth is much more massive than a falling apple, only the Earth exerts a force on the apple, and the apple exerts no force on the Earth. Explain, using Newton's law of gravitation and Newton's third law, why this reasoning is incorrect.

**Answer: By Newton's third law, the apple exerts an equal and opposite gravitational force on the Earth — the forces are equal in magnitude, but because the Earth's mass is enormously larger, the resulting acceleration of the Earth (a=F/m) is imperceptibly tiny compared to the apple's acceleration, creating the illusion that only the Earth "acts."**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

Newton's law of gravitation, F = Gm₁m₂/r², is inherently symmetric in m₁ and m₂ — it does not distinguish which mass is "doing the attracting." The gravitational force the Earth exerts on the apple has exactly the same magnitude as the force the apple exerts on the Earth, consistent with Newton's third law (action-reaction pairs are always equal and opposite). However, acceleration depends on both force and mass (a = F/m). Since the apple's mass is minuscule compared to Earth's mass, the same force magnitude produces a large, easily observable acceleration in the apple (it visibly falls) but an utterly negligible acceleration in the Earth (Earth's mass is so large that F/M_Earth is effectively zero for practical purposes). This is why we perceive only the apple as "moving toward" the Earth, even though technically both bodies accelerate toward each other.

</details>

**Q2 (Mathematical):** Find the gravitational force of attraction between two objects of mass 200 kg and 500 kg separated by a distance of 10 m. (G = 6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²)

**Answer: F ≈ 6.674×10⁻⁸ N**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

F = Gm₁m₂/r² = (6.674×10⁻¹¹ × 200 × 500)/(10²) = (6.674×10⁻¹¹ × 100000)/100 = (6.674×10⁻⁶)/100 = 6.674×10⁻⁸ N

</details>

**Q3 (Mathematical, board-difficulty 2024–2026 style):** Two identical spheres, each of mass 40 kg, are placed such that the gravitational force between them is 2.669×10⁻⁷ N. Find the distance between their centers. (G = 6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²)

**Answer: r ≈ 2 m**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

F = Gm₁m₂/r² → r² = Gm₁m₂/F

r² = (6.674×10⁻¹¹ × 40 × 40)/(2.669×10⁻⁷)
r² = (6.674×10⁻¹¹ × 1600)/(2.669×10⁻⁷)
r² = (1.0678×10⁻⁷)/(2.669×10⁻⁷)
r² ≈ 4.0
r ≈ 2 m

</details>
