# Chapter 5: Work, Energy and Power
## Micro-Topic 9: Coefficient of Restitution (সংঘর্ষের পুনরুদ্ধার সহগ)

---

### 1. Topic Identification & Core Theory (NCTB Standard)

**Micro-Topic Name:** Coefficient of Restitution — Quantifying the Elasticity of a Collision (সংঘর্ষের স্থিতিস্থাপকতার পরিমাণগত পরিমাপ)

**Deep-Dive Explanation:**

The coefficient of restitution (e) is a dimensionless quantity that measures how elastic a given collision actually is, ranging continuously between the two idealized extremes covered in the previous micro-topic. [পুনরুদ্ধার সহগ (e) একটি মাত্রাহীন রাশি যা একটি নির্দিষ্ট সংঘর্ষ প্রকৃতপক্ষে কতটা স্থিতিস্থাপক তা পরিমাপ করে, যা পূর্ববর্তী মাইক্রো-টপিকে আলোচিত দুটি আদর্শ চরম সীমার মধ্যে ক্রমাগতভাবে পরিবর্তিত হয়।]
It is defined as the ratio of the relative velocity of separation of the two bodies after collision to the relative velocity of approach before collision. [এটি সংজ্ঞায়িত করা হয় সংঘর্ষের পরে দুই বস্তুর আপেক্ষিক পৃথকীকরণ বেগ এবং সংঘর্ষের আগে আপেক্ষিক নিকটবর্তী হওয়ার বেগের অনুপাত হিসেবে।]
For a perfectly elastic collision, e = 1, meaning the bodies separate with exactly the same relative speed they approached with (no kinetic energy lost). [একটি পূর্ণ স্থিতিস্থাপক সংঘর্ষের জন্য, e = 1, অর্থাৎ বস্তুগুলো ঠিক একই আপেক্ষিক বেগে পৃথক হয় যে বেগে তারা কাছাকাছি এসেছিল (কোনো গতিশক্তি ক্ষয় হয় না)।]
For a perfectly inelastic collision, e = 0, meaning the bodies have zero relative velocity after collision — they move together as one, which is consistent with the "sticking together" condition from Micro-Topic 8. [একটি পূর্ণ অস্থিতিস্থাপক সংঘর্ষের জন্য, e = 0, অর্থাৎ সংঘর্ষের পরে বস্তুগুলোর আপেক্ষিক বেগ শূন্য — তারা একসাথে একক বস্তু হিসেবে চলে, যা মাইক্রো-টপিক ৮-এর "একসাথে লেগে যাওয়া" শর্তের সাথে সামঞ্জস্যপূর্ণ।]
All real-world collisions fall somewhere between these extremes, with 0 < e < 1, and the value of e depends on the materials and physical properties of the colliding bodies (e.g., a rubber ball has a higher e against a hard floor than a clay ball does). [সকল বাস্তব সংঘর্ষ এই দুই চরম সীমার মধ্যে কোথাও পড়ে, 0 < e < 1, এবং e-এর মান সংঘর্ষরত বস্তুগুলোর উপাদান ও ভৌত ধর্মের উপর নির্ভর করে (যেমন, একটি রাবার বলের e একটি শক্ত মেঝের বিপরীতে একটি কাদামাটির বলের চেয়ে বেশি)।]
The coefficient of restitution provides an experimentally measurable way to characterize a collision without needing to directly measure energy loss — a practical, testable quantity in laboratory settings (e.g., the classic bouncing-ball-height experiment). [পুনরুদ্ধার সহগ একটি পরীক্ষামূলকভাবে পরিমাপযোগ্য উপায় প্রদান করে যা দিয়ে সরাসরি শক্তি ক্ষয় পরিমাপ না করেই একটি সংঘর্ষকে বৈশিষ্ট্যায়িত করা যায় — যা পরীক্ষাগারে একটি ব্যবহারিক, পরীক্ষণযোগ্য রাশি (যেমন ক্লাসিক বল-বাউন্স-উচ্চতা পরীক্ষা)।]

**Multi-Book Synthesis:**

- **Tapan Sir (Hasan Book House):** Frames the coefficient of restitution as the natural bridge concept connecting the two idealized collision types from Micro-Topic 8 into a single unified, measurable spectrum, and emphasizes that e is a property of the *pair of materials in contact*, not of either object alone — a frequent conceptual clarification point in CQ answers.
- **Ishaq & Amir Hossain Khan Sir (Ideal Books):** Provide the formal derivation connecting e to the classic "bouncing ball" experiment: e = √(h₂/h₁), where h₁ is the drop height and h₂ is the rebound height, derived using conservation of mechanical energy (Micro-Topic 6) applied separately before and after impact — this is the standard board-tested numerical application.
- **Akkharpatra Physics:** Offers extensive application variety, including multi-bounce problems (finding rebound height after several successive bounces using repeated application of e), and problems connecting e to fractional kinetic energy loss in a single collision, a favored higher-order CQ style in recent board sets.

### 2. Mathematical Framework & Formulas

**Primary Definition:**

$$e = \frac{\text{relative velocity of separation}}{\text{relative velocity of approach}} = \frac{v_2 - v_1}{u_1 - u_2}$$

**Bouncing Ball Special Case (drop height h₁, rebound height h₂):**

$$e = \sqrt{\frac{h_2}{h_1}}$$

**Multi-Bounce Extension (rebound height after n bounces, from initial drop height h₀):**

$$h_n = e^{2n}h_0$$

| Symbol | Meaning | SI Unit | Physical Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| e | Coefficient of restitution | Dimensionless [মাত্রাহীন] | Measure of collision elasticity, 0 ≤ e ≤ 1 [সংঘর্ষের স্থিতিস্থাপকতার পরিমাপ] |
| u₁, u₂ | Initial velocities before collision | m/s [মিটার/সেকেন্ড] | Approach velocities [নিকটবর্তী হওয়ার বেগ] |
| v₁, v₂ | Final velocities after collision | m/s [মিটার/সেকেন্ড] | Separation velocities [পৃথকীকরণ বেগ] |
| h₁ | Drop height | Metre (m) [মিটার] | Height from which object is dropped [যে উচ্চতা থেকে বস্তু ফেলা হয়] |
| h₂ | Rebound height | Metre (m) [মিটার] | Height object rebounds to after impact [সংঘর্ষের পর বস্তু যে উচ্চতায় ওঠে] |

**Common Traps (English Version students):**
1. **Forgetting the square root in the height formula:** Students often write e = h₂/h₁ directly instead of the correct e = √(h₂/h₁) — this is because e relates to *velocity* ratios, and velocity relates to height via v = √(2gh), introducing the square root.
2. **Sign errors in the velocity-ratio definition:** Getting the numerator and denominator reversed, or mismatching which velocity belongs to which body in multi-body problems — always double-check that separation velocity (after) is on top, approach velocity (before) is on bottom.
3. **Treating e as a fixed universal constant:** Assuming a single object always has "one" coefficient of restitution regardless of what it collides with — e is a property of the *pair* of surfaces/materials in contact, and changes if the collision partner changes (e.g., a ball's e differs on concrete vs. carpet).
4. **Incorrectly applying e = 1 or e = 0 without justification:** Assuming a collision is perfectly elastic or perfectly inelastic without the problem stating so explicitly — board numerical problems usually specify e directly or provide enough data (like heights) to calculate it.

### 3. Chronological Type-Wise Board Questions (2016 – 2026)

⚠️ Same note as previous micro-topics: board-pattern representative problems, not guaranteed verbatim board transcriptions.

**Type A: Direct Substitution (Bouncing Ball, Height Ratio)**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** A ball is dropped from a height of 5 m and rebounds to a height of 3.2 m. Find the coefficient of restitution between the ball and the floor.
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. Formula: e = √(h₂/h₁) [সূত্র প্রয়োগ]
  2. e = √(3.2/5) = √0.64 = **0.8**
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** Step 2 requires recognizing 0.64 as a perfect square for a clean board-friendly answer — a common numerical design pattern in board questions on this topic. [ধাপ ২ এ 0.64-কে একটি পূর্ণবর্গ হিসেবে চেনা প্রয়োজন, যা এই বিষয়ে বোর্ড প্রশ্নের একটি সাধারণ সাংখ্যিক নকশা।]

**Type B: Reverse Application (Find Rebound Height from e)**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** A ball with coefficient of restitution 0.6 is dropped from a height of 2.5 m. Find the height to which it rebounds.
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. e² = h₂/h₁ → h₂ = e² × h₁ [সূত্র পুনর্বিন্যাস]
  2. h₂ = (0.6)² × 2.5 = 0.36 × 2.5 = **0.9 m**
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** Step 1 tests the algebraic rearrangement skill — squaring both sides of e = √(h₂/h₁) to isolate h₂, a frequently tested reverse-application variant of the direct formula. [ধাপ ১ বীজগাণিতিক পুনর্বিন্যাস দক্ষতা পরীক্ষা করে।]

**Type C: Two-Body Collision (Velocity Ratio Definition)**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** A ball moving at 10 m/s collides head-on with a stationary ball and rebounds with a relative separation velocity such that the coefficient of restitution is 0.5. If the stationary ball moves off at 4 m/s after collision, find the velocity of the first ball after collision.
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. Formula: e = (v₂ − v₁)/(u₁ − u₂), with u₂ = 0 [সূত্র প্রয়োগ, স্থির বস্তুর প্রাথমিক বেগ শূন্য]
  2. 0.5 = (4 − v₁)/(10 − 0) → 0.5 × 10 = 4 − v₁ → 5 = 4 − v₁
  3. **v₁ = −1 m/s** (the first ball rebounds backward at 1 m/s)
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** Step 3's negative result must be interpreted physically (rebound direction), not dismissed as an error — a conceptual checkpoint frequently tested alongside the numerical calculation. [ধাপ ৩-এর ঋণাত্মক ফলাফল ভৌতভাবে ব্যাখ্যা করতে হবে (পশ্চাদমুখী গতি), ভুল হিসেবে বাতিল করা যাবে না।]

### 4. Micro-Topic Formative Exercise

**Q1 (Conceptual CQ style):** A student measures the coefficient of restitution of a tennis ball bouncing on a wooden floor and gets e = 0.75. They then claim this value will also apply when the same ball bounces on a concrete floor. Explain why this assumption is likely incorrect.

**Answer: The coefficient of restitution is a property of the specific pair of materials in contact during the collision (the ball AND the surface together), not an intrinsic property of the ball alone — since concrete and wood have different elastic properties, energy dissipation during impact will differ, so e is expected to change with the new surface.**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

The coefficient of restitution arises from how much kinetic energy is converted into other forms (heat, sound, deformation) during the brief contact period of a collision — this conversion depends on the combined material properties of both objects in contact, including their stiffness, damping characteristics, and how they deform and recover. A wooden floor and a concrete floor generally have different stiffness and energy-absorption characteristics, meaning the amount of energy dissipated when the tennis ball strikes each surface will generally differ, even with the same ball. Therefore, e = 0.75 measured on wood cannot be reliably assumed to hold on concrete; a separate experimental measurement would be needed for the new surface.

</details>

**Q2 (Mathematical):** A steel ball is dropped from a height of 1.8 m onto a hard floor and rebounds to a height of 1.152 m. Find the coefficient of restitution.

**Answer: e = 0.8**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

e = √(h₂/h₁) = √(1.152/1.8) = √0.64 = 0.8

</details>

**Q3 (Mathematical, board-difficulty 2024–2026 style):** A ball with a coefficient of restitution of 0.5 is dropped from a height of 4 m onto the ground. Find the total height it will have risen to after the 3rd bounce.

**Answer: h₃ = 0.0625 m**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

Using the multi-bounce formula: hₙ = e^(2n) × h₀

For n = 3: h₃ = (0.5)^(2×3) × 4 = (0.5)^6 × 4 = 0.015625 × 4 = 0.0625 m

Alternatively, step-by-step: h₁ = e²h₀ = 0.25×4 = 1 m; h₂ = e²h₁ = 0.25×1 = 0.25 m; h₃ = e²h₂ = 0.25×0.25 = 0.0625 m — confirming the same result.

</details>
