# Chapter 5: Work, Energy and Power
## Micro-Topic 4: Kinetic Energy (গতিশক্তি)

---

### 1. Topic Identification & Core Theory (NCTB Standard)

**Micro-Topic Name:** Kinetic Energy — Definition, Derivation, and Nature as a State Function (গতিশক্তির সংজ্ঞা, প্রতিপাদন ও অবস্থা-নির্ভর রাশি হিসেবে প্রকৃতি)

**Deep-Dive Explanation:**

Kinetic energy is the energy possessed by a body due to its motion. [গতিশক্তি হলো কোনো বস্তুর গতির কারণে অর্জিত শক্তি।]
It is formally derived by considering the work required to accelerate a body from rest to a given speed v, which by the work-energy theorem (Micro-Topic 3) equals ½mv². [একটি বস্তুকে স্থির অবস্থা থেকে বেগ v পর্যন্ত ত্বরান্বিত করতে প্রয়োজনীয় কাজ বিবেচনা করে এটি প্রতিপাদন করা হয়, যা কাজ-শক্তি উপপাদ্য (মাইক্রো-টপিক ৩) অনুযায়ী ½mv²-এর সমান।]
Unlike work, which is a *process* quantity depending on the path and forces involved, kinetic energy is a *state* quantity — it depends only on the current mass and speed of the body, not on how that speed was achieved. [কাজের বিপরীতে, যা পথ ও বলের উপর নির্ভরশীল একটি প্রক্রিয়া-রাশি, গতিশক্তি একটি অবস্থা-রাশি — এটি কেবল বস্তুর বর্তমান ভর ও বেগের উপর নির্ভর করে, বেগটি কীভাবে অর্জিত হয়েছে তার উপর নয়।]
Kinetic energy is always a non-negative scalar quantity, since it depends on v² (mass and v² are both always positive or zero). [গতিশক্তি সর্বদা একটি অ-ঋণাত্মক স্কেলার রাশি, কারণ এটি v²-এর উপর নির্ভরশীল (ভর ও v² সর্বদা ধনাত্মক বা শূন্য)।]
Because KE depends on v² rather than v, doubling the speed of a body quadruples its kinetic energy — this non-linear relationship is a critical exam-tested insight, especially in vehicle safety/collision contexts. [যেহেতু KE বেগের উপর নির্ভর করে v²-এর মাধ্যমে, বেগ দ্বিগুণ করলে গতিশক্তি চারগুণ হয়ে যায় — এই অরৈখিক সম্পর্কটি বিশেষভাবে যানবাহন নিরাপত্তা/সংঘর্ষ সংক্রান্ত প্রশ্নে গুরুত্বপূর্ণ।]

**Multi-Book Synthesis:**

- **Tapan Sir (Hasan Book House):** Emphasizes the conceptual distinction between kinetic energy (a state function, defined at an instant) and work (a process quantity, defined over an interval) as the single most important idea in this micro-topic, since board CQ questions frequently probe this distinction directly.
- **Ishaq & Amir Hossain Khan Sir (Ideal Books):** Present the full derivation using the work-energy theorem with v_i = 0, treating KE as the specific case "work done to bring a body from rest to speed v," and provide the standard relationship KE = p²/2m (in terms of momentum p = mv) as an important alternate formula frequently used in momentum-linked numerical problems.
- **Akkharpatra Physics:** Provides application variety — comparative KE problems (e.g., bullet vs truck at different speeds), and real-world contextual questions linking KE to road safety and stopping-distance implications, a recurring CQ theme in recent (2023–2026) papers.

### 2. Mathematical Framework & Formulas

**Primary Formula:**

$$KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$$

**Alternate Form (in terms of momentum p = mv):**

$$KE = \frac{p^2}{2m}$$

| Symbol | Meaning | SI Unit | Physical Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| KE | Kinetic energy | Joule (J) [জুল] | Energy due to motion, always ≥ 0 [গতির কারণে শক্তি, সর্বদা ≥ 0] |
| m | Mass of the body | Kilogram (kg) [কিলোগ্রাম] | Inertial mass involved in motion [গতিতে জড়িত জড় ভর] |
| v | Speed of the body | m/s [মিটার/সেকেন্ড] | Instantaneous speed at the moment considered [বিবেচিত মুহূর্তে তাৎক্ষণিক বেগ] |
| p | Linear momentum | kg·m/s [কেজি·মি/সে] | Product of mass and velocity [ভর ও বেগের গুণফল] |

**Common Traps (English Version students):**
1. **Linear vs quadratic confusion:** Assuming KE doubles when v doubles — the correct relationship is KE ∝ v², so doubling v quadruples KE. This is one of the most frequently tested conceptual MCQ traps.
2. **Forgetting KE is always non-negative:** Unlike work (which can be negative), students sometimes incorrectly assign a negative sign to kinetic energy when a body is decelerating — only ΔKE (the *change*) can be negative, never KE itself.
3. **Unit mixing in the momentum-form formula:** Using p in non-SI units (e.g., g·cm/s) directly in KE = p²/2m without conversion produces an incorrect order of magnitude.
4. **Confusing KE with work-energy theorem statement:** KE is a standalone quantity that exists for a body at rest-to-motion state; the work-energy theorem is the *relationship* connecting work to the *change* in this quantity — these are related but distinct ideas, and CQ answers should not conflate the two definitions.

### 3. Chronological Type-Wise Board Questions (2016 – 2026)

⚠️ Same note as previous micro-topics: board-pattern representative problems, not guaranteed verbatim board transcriptions.

**Type A: Direct Substitution**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** Find the kinetic energy of a 1500 kg car moving at 20 m/s.
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. Formula: KE = ½mv² [সূত্র প্রয়োগ]
  2. KE = ½ × 1500 × 20² = ½ × 1500 × 400 = **300000 J = 300 kJ**
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** Step 2 highlights the importance of squaring v *before* multiplying by ½m, a common order-of-operations slip under exam time pressure. [ধাপ ২ এ ½m দিয়ে গুণ করার আগে v-কে বর্গ করার গুরুত্ব দেখানো হয়েছে, যা পরীক্ষার সময়ের চাপে একটি সাধারণ ক্রম-সংক্রান্ত ভুল।]

**Type B: Ratio-Based (v² dependence)**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** Two cars of equal mass move at speeds in the ratio 2:3. Find the ratio of their kinetic energies.
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. KE ∝ v² (since m is equal for both) [ভর সমান হওয়ায় KE ∝ v²]
  2. KE₁/KE₂ = v₁²/v₂² = 2²/3² = **4:9**
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** Step 2 is the direct test of the quadratic-dependence trap — a linear ratio guess (2:3) is the most common wrong answer among students who have not internalized KE ∝ v². [ধাপ ২ সরাসরি বর্গীয় নির্ভরতার ফাঁদ পরীক্ষা করে — রৈখিক অনুপাত অনুমান (2:3) হলো সবচেয়ে সাধারণ ভুল উত্তর।]

**Type C: Momentum-Linked (KE = p²/2m)**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** A 0.2 kg ball has a linear momentum of 4 kg·m/s. Find its kinetic energy.
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. Formula: KE = p²/2m [সূত্র প্রয়োগ]
  2. KE = 4²/(2 × 0.2) = 16/0.4 = **40 J**
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** This question type tests whether students recognize that KE can be found without ever computing velocity explicitly, using the momentum-form shortcut — useful when momentum is given directly rather than mass and velocity separately. [এই প্রশ্নের ধরনটি পরীক্ষা করে যে শিক্ষার্থীরা বেগ সরাসরি নির্ণয় না করেই ভরবেগ-রূপ সূত্র ব্যবহার করে KE নির্ণয় করতে পারে কিনা।]

### 4. Micro-Topic Formative Exercise

**Q1 (Conceptual CQ style):** A truck and a motorcycle are moving with the same kinetic energy. Explain, using the KE formula, why the motorcycle (having much smaller mass) must be moving significantly faster than the truck, and discuss the safety implications of this fact.

**Answer: Since KE = ½mv², a smaller mass requires a proportionally larger v² (and thus much higher speed) to achieve the same KE as a larger mass — meaning the motorcycle must travel much faster, but its stopping/impact energy is equally hazardous.**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

If KE_truck = KE_motorcycle, then ½m_truck·v_truck² = ½m_motorcycle·v_motorcycle². Since m_truck ≫ m_motorcycle, it follows that v_motorcycle² ≫ v_truck², meaning v_motorcycle ≫ v_truck. Because of the square-root (not direct proportional) relationship, the motorcycle's speed must be higher by a factor of √(m_truck/m_motorcycle), not simply the mass ratio itself. This is significant for road safety: a lighter vehicle moving fast enough can carry the same destructive kinetic energy as a heavier vehicle moving slower, so speed limits differentiated by vehicle type must account for this quadratic relationship, not a linear one.

</details>

**Q2 (Mathematical):** A bullet of mass 0.02 kg is fired with a kinetic energy of 900 J. Find its speed.

**Answer: v = 300 m/s**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

KE = ½mv² → 900 = ½ × 0.02 × v² → 900 = 0.01 v² → v² = 90000 → v = 300 m/s

</details>

**Q3 (Mathematical, board-difficulty 2024–2026 style):** A 60 kg cyclist increases speed from 4 m/s to 10 m/s. Find (a) the increase in kinetic energy, and (b) the net work done on the cyclist-bicycle system during this speed change (assume total moving mass = 60 kg).

**Answer: (a) ΔKE = 2520 J, (b) W_net = 2520 J**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

KE_initial = ½ × 60 × 4² = ½ × 60 × 16 = 480 J
KE_final = ½ × 60 × 10² = ½ × 60 × 100 = 3000 J
ΔKE = 3000 − 480 = 2520 J

By the work-energy theorem (Micro-Topic 3), W_net = ΔKE, so the net work done on the system is also 2520 J.

</details>
