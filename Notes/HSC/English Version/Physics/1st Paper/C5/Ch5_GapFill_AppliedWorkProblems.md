### 1. Topic Identification & Core Theory (NCTB Standard)

**Micro-Topic Name:** Four Standard Applied-Work Problem Types Using W = Fs cosθ and W = mgh (মহাকর্ষ, পানি উত্তোলন, ইট স্তূপকরণ ও পেরেক-হাতুড়ি সমস্যার চারটি আদর্শ ধরন)

**Deep-Dive Explanation:**

These four problem types are direct applications of the work formula already established (Micro-Topics 1 and 5 of the original sequence) but require an additional problem-specific setup step before the formula can be applied. [এই চারটি প্রশ্নের ধরন ইতিমধ্যে প্রতিষ্ঠিত কাজের সূত্রের (মূল ক্রমের মাইক্রো-টপিক ১ ও ৫) সরাসরি প্রয়োগ, তবে সূত্র প্রয়োগের আগে একটি অতিরিক্ত সমস্যা-নির্দিষ্ট প্রস্তুতিমূলক ধাপ প্রয়োজন।]
**Work done against gravity (raising/lowering a body)** uses W = mgh directly when a single mass moves a fixed vertical height, but becomes more complex when the mass or height varies during the process — such as lifting water from a well as the water level drops, or stacking bricks where each brick is lifted to a different height. [মহাকর্ষের বিরুদ্ধে কৃতকাজ (বস্তু উত্তোলন/নামানো) সরাসরি W = mgh ব্যবহার করে যখন একটি একক ভর নির্দিষ্ট উল্লম্ব উচ্চতায় সরে, কিন্তু আরও জটিল হয়ে ওঠে যখন প্রক্রিয়ার সময় ভর বা উচ্চতা পরিবর্তিত হয় — যেমন কূপ থেকে পানি তোলার সময় পানির স্তর নেমে যাওয়া, বা ইট স্তূপ করা যেখানে প্রতিটি ইট ভিন্ন উচ্চতায় ওঠানো হয়।]
**Water-lifting from a well/tank** requires calculating the work done to raise the *entire mass of water* through an *effective average height*, since different portions of water travel different distances — the correct approach uses the height risen by the center of mass of the water (typically half the total depth if starting empty). [কূপ/ট্যাংকি থেকে পানি তোলার জন্য পানির *সম্পূর্ণ ভর*কে একটি *কার্যকর গড় উচ্চতা* পর্যন্ত তোলার কাজ হিসাব করতে হয়, কারণ পানির বিভিন্ন অংশ বিভিন্ন দূরত্ব অতিক্রম করে — সঠিক পদ্ধতি পানির ভরকেন্দ্রের উত্থিত উচ্চতা ব্যবহার করে (সাধারণত মোট গভীরতার অর্ধেক, যদি খালি থেকে শুরু হয়)।]
**Brick-stacking (building a wall/stack)** similarly requires summing the work needed to lift each successive brick (or layer) to its own specific height, which — for n identical bricks stacked to height h — reduces to using the center-of-mass height of the entire stack (h/2 for a uniform stack built from the ground). [ইটের স্তূপ গড়া (দেয়াল/স্তূপ তৈরি) একইভাবে প্রতিটি ধারাবাহিক ইট (বা স্তর)-কে তার নিজস্ব নির্দিষ্ট উচ্চতায় তোলার জন্য প্রয়োজনীয় কাজ যোগ করতে হয়, যা — উচ্চতা h পর্যন্ত স্তূপীকৃত n সংখ্যক অভিন্ন ইটের জন্য — সম্পূর্ণ স্তূপের ভরকেন্দ্র-উচ্চতা ব্যবহার করে সরলীকৃত হয় (মাটি থেকে তৈরি একটি সুষম স্তূপের জন্য h/2)।]
**Hammer-nail problems** apply the work-energy theorem (Micro-Topic 3 of the original sequence): the kinetic energy of the moving hammer just before impact is converted into the work done in driving the nail into the material against a resistive force, allowing the resistive (average) force or penetration depth to be calculated. [হাতুড়ি-পেরেক সমস্যা কাজ-শক্তি উপপাদ্য (মূল ক্রমের মাইক্রো-টপিক ৩) প্রয়োগ করে: সংঘর্ষের ঠিক আগে চলমান হাতুড়ির গতিশক্তি উপাদানে প্রতিরোধী বলের বিরুদ্ধে পেরেক ঢোকাতে কৃত কাজে রূপান্তরিত হয়, যা প্রতিরোধী (গড়) বল বা প্রবেশ গভীরতা নির্ণয় করতে দেয়।]

**Multi-Book Synthesis:**

- **Tapan Sir (Hasan Book House):** Frames all four as "composite/multi-step work problems" that test whether students can correctly identify the *effective displacement* (often the center-of-mass height, not the total distance) before applying W = mgh or W = Fs cosθ — emphasized as a distinct problem-solving skill beyond simple formula substitution.
- **Ishaq & Amir Hossain Khan Sir (Ideal Books):** Provide the formal center-of-mass derivation for the water-lifting and brick-stacking cases (integrating small work contributions dW = ρg·A·y·dy for water, or dW = (m/n)g·y_k for each brick layer, then summing/integrating to get total W), and treat the hammer-nail problem as a standard work-energy theorem application with constant resistive force assumption.
- **Akkharpatra Physics:** Offers extensive application variety across all four sub-types — varying well depths and pump efficiencies, multi-layer brick/stone stacking with non-uniform layer masses, and multi-blow hammer-nail problems (where energy per blow determines total penetration after several strikes), a favored complex applied-CQ style in recent board sets.

### 2. Mathematical Framework & Formulas

**Work Against Gravity (single mass, fixed height):**

$$W = mgh$$

**Water-Lifting from a Well/Tank (starting empty, filled to depth H, water raised to the top):**

$$W = \frac{1}{2}Mgh \quad \text{(center-of-mass effective height} = h/2\text{, for water lifted from a full column of depth } h\text{)}$$

*(Note: exact setup depends on whether the well is being emptied from a fixed water column, or water is pumped in at a fixed rate to a fixed height — always identify which scenario the specific problem describes.)*

**Brick-Stacking (n identical bricks, each of mass m₀, stacked to total height h, uniform layers):**

$$W = \frac{1}{2}(nm_0)gh = \frac{1}{2}Mgh \quad \text{(where } M = nm_0 \text{ is total mass, using center-of-mass height } h/2\text{)}$$

**Hammer-Nail (Work-Energy Theorem application):**

$$\frac{1}{2}mv^2 = F_{resistive} \times d \quad \implies \quad F_{resistive} = \frac{mv^2}{2d}$$

| Symbol | Meaning | SI Unit | Physical Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| M | Total mass (water/bricks) | Kilogram (kg) [কিলোগ্রাম] | Combined mass of all lifted material [উত্তোলিত সমস্ত উপাদানের সম্মিলিত ভর] |
| h | Total height/depth of the column or stack | Metre (m) [মিটার] | Full vertical extent involved [সম্পূর্ণ উল্লম্ব বিস্তার] |
| m | Mass of hammer (or striking object) | Kilogram (kg) [কিলোগ্রাম] | Mass delivering kinetic energy on impact [সংঘর্ষে গতিশক্তি সরবরাহকারী ভর] |
| v | Velocity of hammer just before impact | m/s [মিটার/সেকেন্ড] | Impact speed determining available KE [প্রাপ্য KE নির্ধারণকারী সংঘর্ষ বেগ] |
| d | Penetration depth (nail driven into material) | Metre (m) [মিটার] | Distance over which resistive force acts [যে দূরত্বে প্রতিরোধী বল কাজ করে] |
| F_resistive | Average resistive force of the material | Newton (N) [নিউটন] | Opposing force absorbing the hammer's KE [হাতুড়ির KE শোষণকারী প্রতিরোধী বল] |

**Common Traps (English Version students):**
1. **Using total height instead of center-of-mass (half) height for distributed masses:** The single most common error across water-lifting and brick-stacking problems — using W = Mgh directly (full height) instead of W = ½Mgh (center-of-mass height), when the mass is distributed from the bottom to the top rather than being a single point mass lifted the full distance.
2. **Misreading the water-lifting scenario:** Failing to distinguish between (a) water already sitting in a well/tank that must be pumped out to the top [uses ½Mgh], versus (b) water being pumped from a fixed-depth source and delivered continuously to a fixed height [uses Mgh directly, since every unit of water travels the same full height] — these are physically different setups requiring different formulas.
3. **Ignoring multiple hammer blows:** When a problem states the nail is driven in by multiple successive hammer strikes, students sometimes apply the single-blow formula only once instead of correctly summing/averaging the energy delivered across all blows to find total penetration.
4. **Forgetting that "resistive force" in the hammer-nail case is typically assumed constant (average) unless stated otherwise:** This is a simplifying assumption that should be explicitly acknowledged in board CQ answers, since real resistive force may vary with penetration depth.

### 3. Chronological Type-Wise Board Questions (2016 – 2026)

⚠️ Board-pattern representative problems, not guaranteed verbatim board transcriptions.

**Type A: Water-Lifting (Well/Tank, Center-of-Mass Height)**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** A cylindrical well of depth 10 m is completely full of water. Find the work done in pumping all the water to the top. Total mass of water = 8000 kg. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. Since water fills the well uniformly from bottom to top, use center-of-mass height = h/2 = 10/2 = 5 m [ভরকেন্দ্র উচ্চতা নির্ণয়]
  2. W = Mg(h/2) = 8000 × 9.8 × 5 = **392000 J = 392 kJ**
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** Step 1 is the core conceptual test of this problem type — recognizing that water at the bottom must travel the full 10 m while water at the top travels ~0 m, so the *average* (center-of-mass) distance traveled by the water mass is 5 m, not 10 m. [ধাপ ১ এই প্রশ্নের ধরনের মূল ধারণাগত পরীক্ষা।]

**Type B: Brick-Stacking**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** 500 identical bricks, each of mass 2 kg, are used to build a wall of height 3 m, with bricks stacked uniformly from the ground up. Find the work done against gravity in building the wall. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. Total mass M = 500 × 2 = 1000 kg [মোট ভর নির্ণয়]
  2. Using center-of-mass height = h/2 = 3/2 = 1.5 m [ভরকেন্দ্র উচ্চতা]
  3. W = Mg(h/2) = 1000 × 9.8 × 1.5 = **14700 J = 14.7 kJ**
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** Step 2 mirrors the water-lifting logic — since bricks are distributed uniformly from ground level to the top of the wall, the average brick travels only half the total wall height, directly reinforcing Common Trap #1. [ধাপ ২ পানি-উত্তোলনের যুক্তির প্রতিফলন।]

**Type C: Hammer-Nail (Work-Energy Theorem)**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** A hammer of mass 0.5 kg strikes a nail at a speed of 6 m/s and drives it 2 cm into a piece of wood in a single blow. Find the average resistive force exerted by the wood on the nail.
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. KE of hammer just before impact = ½mv² = ½ × 0.5 × 6² = ½ × 0.5 × 36 = 9 J [আঘাতের পূর্বে গতিশক্তি]
  2. This KE is entirely converted to work against the resistive force over depth d = 0.02 m: KE = F × d [কাজ-শক্তি উপপাদ্য প্রয়োগ]
  3. 9 = F × 0.02 → **F = 450 N**
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** Step 2 applies the work-energy theorem in its simplest form — assuming all kinetic energy converts to work against a constant resistive force, a standard simplifying assumption for this problem type (Common Trap #4). [ধাপ ২ সরলতম আকারে কাজ-শক্তি উপপাদ্য প্রয়োগ করে।]

### 4. Micro-Topic Formative Exercise

**Q1 (Conceptual CQ style):** A student calculates the work done to pump water out of a full well of depth h using W = Mgh (full height), arguing that "all the water ends up at height h, so that's the total height it moved." Explain what is wrong with this reasoning.

**Answer: The formula W = Mgh would be correct only if every particle of water started at the very bottom of the well and rose the full height h — but in a well full of water, water near the top only needs to rise a small distance to reach the top, while only the water at the very bottom needs to rise the full h; using the average (center-of-mass) height h/2 correctly accounts for this distribution.**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

The error lies in conflating the *final position* of the water (all of it ends up at the top, height h) with the *distance each particle actually traveled* to get there. Water originally sitting near the top of the well has to travel only a very small vertical distance to reach the top; water at the very bottom must travel the entire depth h. Since work depends on the distance each mass element actually moves (W = ∫mg·dy summed over all water elements), and this distance varies linearly from 0 (at the top) to h (at the bottom), the correct total work is obtained using the average travel distance, which for a uniform distribution is h/2 — equivalent to treating the entire mass of water as if concentrated at its center of mass, then computing the work to raise that single effective point mass by h/2. Using the full height h for the entire mass overestimates the true work by a factor of 2.

</details>

**Q2 (Mathematical):** A 6 m deep tank, initially full of water with total mass 12000 kg, needs to be emptied by pumping all the water to the top. Find the work required. (g = 9.8 m/s²)

**Answer: W = 352800 J = 352.8 kJ**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

W = Mg(h/2) = 12000 × 9.8 × (6/2) = 12000 × 9.8 × 3 = 352800 J = 352.8 kJ

</details>

**Q3 (Mathematical, board-difficulty 2024–2026 style):** A hammer of mass 0.8 kg, moving at 8 m/s, is used to drive a nail into wood. If the wood exerts a constant average resistive force of 800 N on the nail, find (a) the penetration depth from a single blow, and (b) the total penetration depth if the same hammer strikes the nail 3 times in succession at the same speed each time (assume the resistive force stays constant throughout).

**Answer: (a) d = 0.032 m = 3.2 cm, (b) total depth = 0.096 m = 9.6 cm**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

**(a)** KE per blow = ½mv² = ½ × 0.8 × 8² = ½ × 0.8 × 64 = 25.6 J
KE = F × d → 25.6 = 800 × d → d = 25.6/800 = 0.032 m = 3.2 cm

**(b)** Since the resistive force is constant and each blow delivers the same KE (same hammer, same speed each time), each blow drives the nail an additional 3.2 cm:
Total depth = 3 × 3.2 cm = 9.6 cm = 0.096 m

</details>
