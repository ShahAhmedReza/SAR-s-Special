# Chapter 5: Work, Energy and Power
## Micro-Topic 5: Gravitational Potential Energy (মহাকর্ষীয় বিভবশক্তি)

---

### 1. Topic Identification & Core Theory (NCTB Standard)

**Micro-Topic Name:** Gravitational Potential Energy — Definition, Derivation of U = mgh, and Reference Point Dependence (মহাকর্ষীয় বিভবশক্তির সংজ্ঞা, U = mgh প্রতিপাদন ও প্রসঙ্গ বিন্দু নির্ভরতা)

**Deep-Dive Explanation:**

Gravitational potential energy is the energy stored in a body due to its position in a gravitational field, specifically its height above a chosen reference level. [মহাকর্ষীয় বিভবশক্তি হলো কোনো বস্তুর মহাকর্ষ ক্ষেত্রে অবস্থানের কারণে, বিশেষত একটি নির্বাচিত প্রসঙ্গ স্তরের উপরে উচ্চতার কারণে, সঞ্চিত শক্তি।]
It is derived by calculating the work done against gravity to raise a body of mass m to a height h at constant velocity (so kinetic energy does not change). [এটি প্রতিপাদন করা হয় ভর m-এর একটি বস্তুকে ধ্রুব বেগে (যাতে গতিশক্তির পরিবর্তন না হয়) উচ্চতা h পর্যন্ত উত্তোলনে মহাকর্ষের বিরুদ্ধে কৃত কাজ হিসাব করে।]
Since the applied force must equal mg (to balance gravity) and the displacement is h in the same direction as the applied force, the work done against gravity is W = mgh, which is stored as gravitational potential energy U. [যেহেতু প্রযুক্ত বলকে অবশ্যই mg-এর সমান হতে হবে (মহাকর্ষকে ভারসাম্য রাখতে) এবং সরণ h প্রযুক্ত বলের দিকেই হয়, তাই মহাকর্ষের বিরুদ্ধে কৃত কাজ W = mgh, যা মহাকর্ষীয় বিভবশক্তি U হিসেবে সঞ্চিত থাকে।]
Crucially, gravitational potential energy has no absolute value — it is always defined relative to an arbitrarily chosen reference point (often the ground, or the lowest point in a problem), and only *differences* in potential energy (ΔU) have unambiguous physical meaning. [গুরুত্বপূর্ণভাবে, মহাকর্ষীয় বিভবশক্তির কোনো পরম মান নেই — এটি সর্বদা একটি স্বেচ্ছাধীনভাবে নির্বাচিত প্রসঙ্গ বিন্দুর সাপেক্ষে সংজ্ঞায়িত হয়, এবং কেবল বিভবশক্তির পার্থক্য (ΔU) স্পষ্ট ভৌত অর্থ বহন করে।]
This formula U = mgh is valid only near the Earth's surface where g can be treated as approximately constant; for large height variations (e.g., satellites), a different inverse-square-based formula is required, covered in the Gravitation chapter. [U = mgh সূত্রটি কেবল পৃথিবীর পৃষ্ঠের কাছাকাছি বৈধ যেখানে g প্রায় ধ্রুবক ধরা যায়; বড় উচ্চতা পরিবর্তনের ক্ষেত্রে ভিন্ন এক ব্যস্তবর্গীয়-ভিত্তিক সূত্র প্রয়োজন হয়।]

**Multi-Book Synthesis:**

- **Tapan Sir (Hasan Book House):** Strongly emphasizes "reference point is arbitrary, only ΔU matters" as the highest-yield idea for CQ answers, since examiners frequently test this nuance by asking students to justify why the choice of reference point does not affect measurable results.
- **Ishaq & Amir Hossain Khan Sir (Ideal Books):** Provide the formal constant-velocity-lifting derivation (F = mg balancing gravity, W = Fh cos0° = mgh) as the standard reproducible CQ derivation, connecting forward to conservation-of-energy applications.
- **Akkharpatra Physics:** Offers application variety — inclined plane problems (h = L sinθ), multi-level reference point problems, and PE-to-KE conversion scenarios (falling objects, pendulums) as preparation for the next micro-topic.

### 2. Mathematical Framework & Formulas

**Primary Formula:**

$$U = mgh$$

**Inclined Plane Variant (height component from incline length L at angle θ):**

$$U = mg(L\sin\theta)$$

| Symbol | Meaning | SI Unit | Physical Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| U | Gravitational potential energy | Joule (J) [জুল] | Stored energy due to position/height [অবস্থান/উচ্চতার কারণে সঞ্চিত শক্তি] |
| m | Mass of the body | Kilogram (kg) [কিলোগ্রাম] | Amount of matter being raised [উত্তোলিত বস্তুর পরিমাণ] |
| g | Acceleration due to gravity | m/s² [মিটার/সেকেন্ড²] | Local gravitational field strength (≈9.8 m/s² near Earth's surface) [স্থানীয় মহাকর্ষীয় ক্ষেত্রের প্রাবল্য] |
| h | Height above reference level | Metre (m) [মিটার] | Vertical displacement from the chosen zero-reference point [নির্বাচিত শূন্য-প্রসঙ্গ বিন্দু থেকে উল্লম্ব সরণ] |

**Common Traps (English Version students):**
1. **Forgetting reference-point arbitrariness:** Treating U as absolute rather than realizing only ΔU between two states matters physically — a common CQ error.
2. **Using incline length instead of vertical height:** On inclined-plane problems, mistakenly using slope length L instead of h = L sinθ — a frequent numerical board error.
3. **Sign confusion when object descends:** When a body moves *down* by height h, ΔU = −mgh — students sometimes keep it positive by only considering magnitude.
4. **Assuming g is always 9.8 m/s² without checking the given value:** Some board questions supply a different g (hypothetical planet) — using the memorized Earth value instead is a classic error.

### 3. Chronological Type-Wise Board Questions (2016 – 2026)

⚠️ Same note as previous micro-topics: board-pattern representative problems, not guaranteed verbatim board transcriptions.

**Type A: Direct Substitution**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** Find the gravitational potential energy of a 5 kg object placed at a height of 12 m above the ground. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. Formula: U = mgh [সূত্র প্রয়োগ]
  2. U = 5 × 9.8 × 12 = **588 J**
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** Step 2 is a direct three-quantity multiplication; the main assessed skill is correct unit handling (kg × m/s² × m = J). [ধাপ ২ একটি সরাসরি তিন-রাশির গুণফল; মূল দক্ষতা হলো সঠিক একক ব্যবহার।]

**Type B: Inclined Plane (Height from Slope Length)**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** A 3 kg block is pushed up a frictionless incline of length 8 m, inclined at 30° to the horizontal. Find the increase in gravitational potential energy. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. Vertical height h = L sinθ = 8 × sin30° = 8 × 0.5 = 4 m [উল্লম্ব উচ্চতা নির্ণয়]
  2. ΔU = mgh = 3 × 9.8 × 4 = **117.6 J**
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** Step 1 is critical — students must resolve the actual vertical height from incline length and angle before applying the PE formula, rather than mistakenly using L = 8 m directly. [ধাপ ১ এ সরাসরি L = 8 m ব্যবহার না করে ঢালের দৈর্ঘ্য ও কোণ থেকে প্রকৃত উল্লম্ব উচ্চতা নির্ণয় করতে হবে।]

**Type C: Reference-Point / Ratio-Based**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** An object of mass 2 kg is raised from a height of 3 m to a height of 10 m above the ground. Find the change in its gravitational potential energy. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. ΔU = mg(h₂ − h₁) = 2 × 9.8 × (10 − 3) [উচ্চতার পার্থক্য ব্যবহার]
  2. ΔU = 2 × 9.8 × 7 = **137.2 J**
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** Step 1 reinforces that only the *difference* in height matters physically — this format specifically tests reference-point-independence. [ধাপ ১ পুনরায় নিশ্চিত করে যে কেবল উচ্চতার পার্থক্য ভৌতভাবে গুরুত্বপূর্ণ।]

### 4. Micro-Topic Formative Exercise

**Q1 (Conceptual CQ style):** Two students calculate the potential energy of the same book resting on a table — one measures height from the floor, the other from the tabletop itself (so h = 0 for them). Explain why both students can still correctly agree on the change in potential energy if the book is later dropped to the floor, despite disagreeing on the book's "initial" potential energy value.

**Answer: Because gravitational PE is reference-point dependent, but ΔU between any two states is reference-point independent — both students calculate the same ΔU (and hence the same resulting KE via energy conservation) even though their individual U values differ by a constant offset.**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

Let the floor-referencing student assign U_table = mgh_table and U_floor = 0. The tabletop-referencing student assigns U_table = 0 and U_floor = −mgh_table. Despite disagreeing on individual values, both compute the same magnitude of change: ΔU = −mgh_table in both cases. Since only ΔU (not absolute U) determines observable physics — such as the kinetic energy gained by the book when dropped — both students predict identical final speeds, confirming that the choice of reference point has no physical consequence.

</details>

**Q2 (Mathematical):** A 4 kg object is lifted vertically from the ground to a height of 6 m. Find the work done against gravity. (g = 9.8 m/s²)

**Answer: W = 235.2 J**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

W = mgh = 4 × 9.8 × 6 = 235.2 J

</details>

**Q3 (Mathematical, board-difficulty 2024–2026 style):** A pendulum bob of mass 0.5 kg is pulled aside so that it rises 0.2 m above its lowest point, then released. Using energy considerations (not kinematics), find the speed of the bob as it passes through the lowest point. (g = 9.8 m/s², neglect air resistance)

**Answer: v ≈ 1.98 m/s**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

At the highest point (release), the bob is momentarily at rest: KE = 0, U = mgh = 0.5 × 9.8 × 0.2 = 0.98 J
At the lowest point, all this potential energy converts to kinetic energy: KE = 0.98 J

½mv² = 0.98
½ × 0.5 × v² = 0.98
0.25 v² = 0.98
v² = 3.92
v = √3.92 ≈ 1.98 m/s

This previews the next micro-topic's core idea: conservation of mechanical energy (U_initial + KE_initial = U_final + KE_final).

</details>
