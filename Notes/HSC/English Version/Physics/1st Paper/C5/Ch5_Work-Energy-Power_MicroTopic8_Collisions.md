# Chapter 5: Work, Energy and Power
## Micro-Topic 8: Elastic and Inelastic Collisions (স্থিতিস্থাপক ও অস্থিতিস্থাপক সংঘর্ষ)

---

### 1. Topic Identification & Core Theory (NCTB Standard)

**Micro-Topic Name:** Elastic and Inelastic Collisions — Definitions and Conservation Laws (স্থিতিস্থাপক ও অস্থিতিস্থাপক সংঘর্ষের সংজ্ঞা ও নিত্যতা সূত্র)

**Deep-Dive Explanation:**

A collision is an event in which two or more bodies exert relatively strong forces on each other for a relatively short time. [সংঘর্ষ এমন একটি ঘটনা যেখানে দুই বা ততোধিক বস্তু অপেক্ষাকৃত অল্প সময়ের জন্য একে অপরের উপর অপেক্ষাকৃত শক্তিশালী বল প্রয়োগ করে।]
In every type of collision — elastic or inelastic — the total linear momentum of the system is always conserved, provided no external force acts on the system. [প্রতিটি ধরনের সংঘর্ষে — স্থিতিস্থাপক হোক বা অস্থিতিস্থাপক — সিস্টেমের উপর কোনো বাহ্যিক বল ক্রিয়া না করলে মোট রৈখিক ভরবেগ সর্বদা সংরক্ষিত থাকে।]
A collision is defined as elastic if, in addition to momentum, the total kinetic energy of the system is also conserved — no kinetic energy is converted to heat, sound, or deformation. [একটি সংঘর্ষকে স্থিতিস্থাপক বলা হয় যদি ভরবেগের পাশাপাশি সিস্টেমের মোট গতিশক্তিও সংরক্ষিত থাকে — কোনো গতিশক্তি তাপ, শব্দ বা বিকৃতিতে রূপান্তরিত না হয়।]
A collision is inelastic if kinetic energy is not conserved — some kinetic energy is lost to other forms of energy, even though momentum remains conserved; a perfectly inelastic collision is the extreme case where the colliding bodies stick together and move with a common final velocity. [একটি সংঘর্ষকে অস্থিতিস্থাপক বলা হয় যদি গতিশক্তি সংরক্ষিত না থাকে — কিছু গতিশক্তি অন্য শক্তিতে রূপান্তরিত হয়, যদিও ভরবেগ সংরক্ষিত থাকে; পূর্ণ অস্থিতিস্থাপক সংঘর্ষ হলো চরম ক্ষেত্র যেখানে সংঘর্ষরত বস্তুগুলো একসাথে লেগে একই চূড়ান্ত বেগে চলতে থাকে।]
Real-world macroscopic collisions (billiard balls, cars, sports equipment) are never perfectly elastic in practice — some energy is always lost — but idealized elastic collisions are used at the molecular/atomic scale and as simplifying models in problem-solving. [বাস্তবে বৃহৎ (ম্যাক্রোস্কোপিক) সংঘর্ষগুলো (বিলিয়ার্ড বল, গাড়ি, ক্রীড়া সরঞ্জাম) কখনোই পুরোপুরি স্থিতিস্থাপক হয় না — সবসময় কিছু শক্তি ক্ষয় হয় — কিন্তু আদর্শ স্থিতিস্থাপক সংঘর্ষ আণবিক/পারমাণবিক স্কেলে এবং সমস্যা সমাধানে সরলীকৃত মডেল হিসেবে ব্যবহৃত হয়।]

**Multi-Book Synthesis:**

- **Tapan Sir (Hasan Book House):** Emphasizes the crucial conceptual distinction that momentum conservation applies universally to ALL collision types, while kinetic energy conservation applies ONLY to elastic collisions — this is the single most frequently tested conceptual point in CQ answers, since students often mistakenly assume both quantities are always conserved together.
- **Ishaq & Amir Hossain Khan Sir (Ideal Books):** Provide the full derivation of the 1D elastic collision final-velocity formulas by simultaneously solving the momentum conservation equation (m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂) and the kinetic energy conservation equation, yielding the standard board formulas for v₁ and v₂ in terms of masses and initial velocities.
- **Akkharpatra Physics:** Offers application variety with special-case scenarios — equal masses (complete velocity exchange in elastic collision), one body initially at rest, and perfectly inelastic "stick together" problems — plus real-world contextual CQs (vehicle collisions, ballistic pendulum) common in 2024–2026 board papers.

### 2. Mathematical Framework & Formulas

**Momentum Conservation (always true, any collision type):**

$$m_1u_1 + m_2u_2 = m_1v_1 + m_2v_2$$

**Kinetic Energy Conservation (elastic collisions only):**

$$\frac{1}{2}m_1u_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2u_2^2 = \frac{1}{2}m_1v_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2v_2^2$$

**1D Elastic Collision — Final Velocities (derived formula):**

$$v_1 = \frac{(m_1-m_2)u_1}{m_1+m_2} + \frac{2m_2u_2}{m_1+m_2} \qquad v_2 = \frac{2m_1u_1}{m_1+m_2} + \frac{(m_2-m_1)u_2}{m_1+m_2}$$

**Perfectly Inelastic Collision (bodies stick together, common final velocity v):**

$$v = \frac{m_1u_1 + m_2u_2}{m_1+m_2}$$

| Symbol | Meaning | SI Unit | Physical Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| m₁, m₂ | Masses of the two colliding bodies | Kilogram (kg) [কিলোগ্রাম] | Inertial mass of each body [প্রতিটি বস্তুর জড় ভর] |
| u₁, u₂ | Initial velocities (before collision) | m/s [মিটার/সেকেন্ড] | Velocity just before impact [সংঘর্ষের ঠিক আগে বেগ] |
| v₁, v₂ | Final velocities (after collision) | m/s [মিটার/সেকেন্ড] | Velocity just after impact [সংঘর্ষের ঠিক পরে বেগ] |
| v | Common final velocity (perfectly inelastic case) | m/s [মিটার/সেকেন্ড] | Shared velocity of the combined mass after sticking [একত্রিত ভরের সাধারণ বেগ] |

**Common Traps (English Version students):**
1. **Assuming kinetic energy is conserved in ALL collisions:** The most critical conceptual error — kinetic energy conservation is an *additional*, special condition true only for elastic collisions, not a general law like momentum conservation.
2. **Sign errors with direction:** In 1D collision problems, velocities in opposite directions must be assigned opposite signs (e.g., one positive, one negative) — forgetting this leads to major numerical errors, especially in head-on collision setups.
3. **Using the wrong formula for equal masses:** In an elastic collision between equal masses (m₁ = m�2) with one initially at rest, the moving body stops completely and the stationary body takes on the moving body's exact velocity — students sometimes misapply the general formula instead of recognizing this special-case shortcut.
4. **Confusing perfectly inelastic with all inelastic collisions:** "Perfectly inelastic" specifically means the bodies stick together and move with one common velocity — this is the maximum-energy-loss case, not representative of all inelastic collisions (which merely require *some* KE loss, without necessarily sticking together).

### 3. Chronological Type-Wise Board Questions (2016 – 2026)

⚠️ Same note as previous micro-topics: board-pattern representative problems, not guaranteed verbatim board transcriptions.

**Type A: Perfectly Inelastic Collision (Direct Substitution)**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** A 3 kg object moving at 6 m/s collides head-on with a stationary 2 kg object, and they stick together. Find their common final velocity.
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. Formula: v = (m₁u₁ + m₂u₂)/(m₁+m₂), with u₂ = 0 [সূত্র প্রয়োগ, স্থির বস্তুর বেগ শূন্য]
  2. v = (3×6 + 2×0)/(3+2) = 18/5 = **3.6 m/s**
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** Step 1 correctly sets u₂ = 0 for the stationary object before substitution, a keyword-to-value translation frequently tested at the start of collision problems. [ধাপ ১ এ স্থির বস্তুর জন্য সঠিকভাবে u₂ = 0 বসানো হয়েছে।]

**Type B: Elastic Collision — Equal Masses (Special Case)**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** A 2 kg ball moving at 5 m/s collides elastically head-on with a stationary 2 kg ball. Find the final velocities of both balls.
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. Since m₁ = m₂ and u₂ = 0, apply the special-case rule for elastic collision with equal masses [সমান ভরের বিশেষ নিয়ম প্রয়োগ]
  2. The moving ball transfers its entire velocity to the stationary ball: v₁ = 0, **v₂ = 5 m/s**
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** Step 2 demonstrates the complete velocity exchange phenomenon unique to equal-mass elastic collisions — this shortcut avoids the need for full formula substitution and is specifically tested to check recognition of the special case (Common Trap #3). [ধাপ ২ সমান-ভরের স্থিতিস্থাপক সংঘর্ষে সম্পূর্ণ বেগ বিনিময়ের ঘটনা প্রদর্শন করে।]

**Type C: General Elastic Collision (Unequal Masses, Full Formula)**

- **Question (Board-pattern):** A 4 kg body moving at 6 m/s collides elastically with a stationary 2 kg body. Find the final velocities of both bodies.
- **NCTB Standard Solution:**
  1. v₁ = [(m₁−m₂)u₁ + 2m₂u₂]/(m₁+m₂) = [(4−2)(6) + 0]/(4+2) = 12/6 = **2 m/s**
  2. v₂ = [2m₁u₁ + (m₂−m₁)u₂]/(m₁+m₂) = [2(4)(6) + 0]/(4+2) = 48/6 = **8 m/s**
  3. Verification: momentum before = 4×6 = 24; momentum after = 4×2 + 2×8 = 8+16 = 24 ✓ [ভরবেগ যাচাই]
- **Line-by-Line Breakdown:** Step 3 demonstrates the board-recommended verification technique — checking that total momentum before and after matches, which catches most arithmetic errors before submitting a final answer. [ধাপ ৩ বোর্ড-প্রস্তাবিত যাচাই কৌশল প্রদর্শন করে — মোট ভরবেগ মিলিয়ে দেখা।]

### 4. Micro-Topic Formative Exercise

**Q1 (Conceptual CQ style):** A student claims that since momentum is always conserved in a collision, kinetic energy must also always be conserved, because "energy and momentum are related." Explain precisely why this claim is incorrect, using the distinction between elastic and inelastic collisions.

**Answer: Momentum conservation is a universal law arising from Newton's third law (equal and opposite internal forces during collision), independent of what happens to kinetic energy — kinetic energy conservation is an additional, stricter condition that only holds when no energy is converted to heat, sound, or deformation, which is the defining feature of an elastic (not inelastic) collision.**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

Momentum conservation follows directly from Newton's third law: during any collision, the force body 1 exerts on body 2 is equal and opposite to the force body 2 exerts on body 1, and since these internal forces act for the same time interval (Newton's third law pairs), the impulses cancel, guaranteeing total momentum is unchanged regardless of what type of collision occurs. Kinetic energy, however, has no such automatic protection — during a collision, some kinetic energy can be converted into heat (due to internal friction/deformation of materials), sound (audible collision noise), or permanent deformation (denting) of the colliding bodies. Whether this conversion happens depends on the physical properties of the materials involved (e.g., rubber vs. clay), which is precisely why "elastic" (no KE loss) and "inelastic" (some KE loss) are separate classifications — momentum conservation alone says nothing about which category a given collision falls into.

</details>

**Q2 (Mathematical):** A 5 kg cart moving at 4 m/s collides with a stationary 3 kg cart and they couple together. Find (a) their common velocity, and (b) the kinetic energy lost in the collision.

**Answer: (a) v = 2.5 m/s, (b) KE lost = 15 J**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

(a) v = (m₁u₁ + m₂u₂)/(m₁+m₂) = (5×4 + 3×0)/(5+3) = 20/8 = 2.5 m/s

(b) KE_initial = ½(5)(4²) = ½×5×16 = 40 J
KE_final = ½(5+3)(2.5²) = ½×8×6.25 = 25 J
KE lost = 40 − 25 = 15 J

</details>

**Q3 (Mathematical, board-difficulty 2024–2026 style):** A 1 kg ball moving at 8 m/s collides elastically head-on with a stationary 3 kg ball. Find the final velocities of both balls, and verify that kinetic energy is conserved.

**Answer: v₁ = −4 m/s, v₂ = 4 m/s; KE before = KE after = 32 J**

<details><summary>Click for Solution</summary>

v₁ = [(m₁−m₂)u₁ + 2m₂u₂]/(m₁+m₂) = [(1−3)(8) + 0]/(1+3) = −16/4 = −4 m/s
v₂ = [2m₁u₁ + (m₂−m₁)u₂]/(m₁+m₂) = [2(1)(8) + 0]/(1+3) = 16/4 = 4 m/s

The negative sign for v₁ indicates the 1 kg ball rebounds backward after the collision.

Verification (Kinetic Energy):
KE_before = ½(1)(8²) = 32 J
KE_after = ½(1)(−4)² + ½(3)(4²) = ½(1)(16) + ½(3)(16) = 8 + 24 = 32 J ✓

Kinetic energy is confirmed conserved, consistent with the elastic collision condition.

</details>
